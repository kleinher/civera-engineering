overview:
  - "This project involved the complete structural development of a large multi-storey residential building located in La Plata, Argentina. The building comprises a ground floor, nine upper residential storeys and a roof terrace, with approximately 3,300 m² of structural floor area. Each typical residential floor accommodates five apartments and two elevators."
  - "A major architectural requirement was the use of the ground floor as covered vehicle parking. This required a substantially more open structural arrangement than the residential floors above. A reinforced-concrete transfer structure was therefore developed at first-floor level to support discontinuous upper-storey columns and redistribute their loads toward the reduced number of ground-floor supports."
  - "Circular reinforced-concrete columns were incorporated at parking level to improve vehicle circulation and reduce interference within the parking area."

challenge:
  - "The main engineering challenge resulted from the substantially different structural requirements of the residential floors and the ground-floor parking area. The residential levels required a relatively dense arrangement of vertical supports, while the parking area required larger clear spaces and fewer columns to allow vehicle access and circulation."
  - "As a result, several upper-storey columns could not continue directly through the ground floor to the foundations. Heavily reinforced transfer beams were therefore introduced at first-floor level to receive concentrated column loads and redistribute them toward the reduced number of ground-floor supports."
  - "The design required particular consideration of concentrated reactions, bending and shear demands, structural stiffness, load redistribution, reinforcement congestion, deflection control and constructability. The final structural solution allowed the residential configuration of the upper storeys to be maintained while providing a substantially more open and functional parking level below."
